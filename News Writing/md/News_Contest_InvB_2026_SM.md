## Invitational B Meet · 2026

Leaguetown High School has 871 students enrolled in grades 9-12. Last week when meteorologists announced that temperatures would drop below freezing for the first time this year for five days straight, maintenance workers tested heating units on each of the district's eight campuses to ensure they were working properly. The high school, Evans Elementary and Clint Elementary had units that would run but would not generate any heat. HVAC technicians found major mechanical malfunctions on each of those campuses, including potentially unsafe gas leaks in the furnace units. In addition, one middle school and an elementary school had units that would heat but would emit a powerful odor. Crews tested units for compliance over the summer but otherwise had not run them for an extended period of time since last winter.

After testing and multiple attempts to repair the units in-house and given the age of the equipment, Superintendent Mac Weinberg announced that all HVAC units in the district would need to be repaired or replaced as soon as possible. However, because of the scale of the project and the need to order specific equipment, the project is expected to take six weeks.

Since multiple days of below freezing temperatures have been forecast for the week of Feb. 16-20, Weinberg made the decision to close district schools for that entire week while crews work to make repairs and replace some HVAC systems.

You are writing for the issue of the press to be distributed Feb. 13.

## Invitational B Meet · 2026 News Writing Contest Tips and Sample Story

Contest Director: Give one copy to each judge to use during judging. Also, staple one copy of the contest and one copy of the contest tips to each student's returned entry. The purpose of the tips is to provide immediate feedback to students. However, it is not meant to replace written comments from the judges.

1. Open with the most timely lead, given the current circumstances, probably the future event or events. In this instance, the story will be published before the district closes campuses for a week due to broken HVAC units and below freezing temperatures.

All district schools will be closed next week because of the predicted five days of below freezing temperatures while the district works to repair and replace parts of its HVAC systems.

2. Use direct quotes and use those quotes early in the story. Start with the most important interview to the story. After you introduce quotes, use the transition/quote formula. Be sure you attribute the quote. An attribution should be: subject of attribution followed by the verb of attribution ('Weinberg said,' not 'said Weinberg.') Avoid putting two or more quotes from different people together without placing a transition between them (stacking quotes). Also, make sure transitions provide information to help the story flow, and that the quote does not repeat information revealed in the transition.

Maintenance workers turned on the heat last week ahead of next week's forecast only to find significant equipment malfunctions to the heating units at five out of eight campuses. Units at three campuses would not generate any heat, while two more campuses had units that emitted an unsafe odor.

## direct quote

'We knew the heating and air conditioning units throughout the district would need to be replaced soon, but we just didn't think we'd have it in the budget this year, so we hoped to get one more year out of some of these older units,' Superintendent Mac Weinberg said. 'That decision has turned out to be a mistake.'

3. Use third person. Don't say 'our students' or 'our principal' or 'Some of our students.' Also, do not editorialize. For instance, you should not say 'This idea probably won't work...' or 'The district should ...' without attributing that quote to someone. These statements are personal opinions and cannot be made in a news story.
4.  Note how in the earlier example of transition/quote, each quote is its own paragraph. Journalistic writing consists mostly of one- and two-sentence paragraphs.
5. End with a strong quote that wraps up the story.
