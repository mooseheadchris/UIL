## District Meet · 2026

Leaguetown High School has 4,011 students enrolled in grades 9-12. Principal Sam Lyman suspended junior Kate Harper for three days last fall after she posted three memes on Instagram using photos of Lyman. One meme depicted Lyman as an anime character. Another showed him in a scene from the TV show 'Stranger Things,' and another showed Lyman in a scene from Minecraft. The memes appeared on Harper's personal account in three separate posts on three separate dates.

According to the student code of conduct, students are prohibited from sharing photos that aim to 'demean, embarrass or discredit' students and staff. It also mandates that students follow a philosophy called the 'Leopard Law,' which outlines the district's expectations for respecting students, staff and facilities in the district.

After serving her suspension, Harper hired an attorney from the First Amendment Foundation who began working with her to sue the school district, claiming the suspension violated her First Amendment rights. A trial is scheduled to begin in August. In the meantime, the district hired a third party to send a survey to parents and students asking for feedback on the student code of conduct.

The school board will discuss the survey results at its March 26 meeting. You are writing for the issue of the press to be distributed March 23.

## ■ KATE HARPER, junior

'Mr. Lyman is just so serious all the time. It's not like he did anything to me. I wasn't trying to hurt him or anyone else by posting the images. It was just satire. I was trying to lightly poke fun at his personality a little bit, but he took major offense to it.

'I got called down to Lyman's office, and he lectured me about respect and the Leopard Law. Then he called my mom and told her I was getting suspended. He wrote up a report that said I violated the student code of conduct. I signed it and left. I was in tears leaving his office, but the more I thought about it, the angrier I got. The school has no right to say what goes on my personal social media accounts. That's my business. That night, my parents helped me find an attorney.

'Students have been making fun of their teachers, administrators and their school since the beginning of time. I didn't deserve to be suspended for making some memes. The suspension should be wiped off my record, and I expect to see some major changes to school policy after we go through this process. But this lawsuit isn't about me and my punishment. A school shouldn't get to decide what students are allowed to say and what we aren't allowed to say on our own time. '
