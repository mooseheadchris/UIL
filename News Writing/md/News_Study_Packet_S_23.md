## News Writing

## State Meet · 2023

Leaguetown High School has 980 students enrolled in grades 9-12. On May 12, the district released a statement in an email to parents of varsity swimmers announcing that the fall 2023 season would be canceled after reports of widespread hazing among members of the team. According to the letter from superintendent Jorge Estevez, the district obtained video of a recent incident that occurred in the training facility where multiple varsity swimmers held other students under water in the whirlpool baths for an extended period of time. In the video, which circulated on social media, one student can be seen choking and vomiting after another student releases their shoulders and allows them to get out of the pool. Varsity swimmers were allegedly initiating younger members of the team.

The district shared the video with police. They are still conducting their own investigation. Two seniors have been charged with assault related to the incident in the video.

Administrators have placed head coach Dirk Davenport and assistant coach Maryanne Stephens on unpaid leave pending a formal investigation. Davenport and Stephens both teach in the social studies department in addition to coaching. Under Texas law, any students or adults who have knowledge of hazing activities could be charged with a crime.

Administrators placed six members of the varsity team in the district's Disciplinary Alternative Education Program for the remainder of the school year for their involvement in the hazing incident. Freshman swimmer Nicole Oesterreicher, who was not involved in the hazing, has written a letter to the superintendent and school board and plans to speak at the school board meeting on Monday, May 22, and ask the board to repeal the cancellation of the 2023 varsity swimming season. You are writing for the issue of the Press to be distributed May 19.

## ■ NICOLE OESTERREICHER, freshman swimmer

'I moved to Leaguetown last summer, so I was never hazed when I joined the team, and I had no knowledge of inappropriate activities that were planned for this spring, either. I'm on varsity, but I'm still a freshman. I guess the older players didn't tell me everything.

'What they did was disgusting, and the players who hazed others all deserve whatever punishment they get. However, it is unfair to take away our swimming season next year. Yes, most of the team was involved, but I wasn't. I know two other sophomores who weren't involved, either. We have plenty of eighth grade talent from the middle school that could move up and create a new varsity team next year, not to mention the swimmers who were being hazed. I plan to speak at the school board meeting.

## ■ JORGE ESTEVEZ, superintendent

'Students who don't feel safe can't perform at their highest potential. As a result, we have decided that suspending the swim program for next fall is the best and safest approach for the students in our charge, particularly while the investigation by law enforcement into the alleged hazing continues.

' At this time, evidence presented to the district reveals that this was not an isolated act but rather part of a systematic culture of hazing. This behavior has gone unchecked, possibly for several years, sadly. We know this outcome is unfortunate and some innocent students will be affected, but this is the best way for us to proceed. '

## ■ JENNIFER D'ONOFRIO, principal

' At this time, we are scrambling to find appropriate, long-term substitute teachers to fill in for our swim coaches. ## ■ MAGGIE SNOWDEN, sophomore swimmer

'We were attacked after practice by a group of varsity swimmers. Luckily, they didn't hold me under water for long, but they did hold one of my friends down until she was choking and sick. I'm not sure our coaches knew exactly what was going on, but they definitely knew something was happening because I saw Coach Davenport pop his head in and roll his eyes.

'That being said, now I'm the one being punished by not being able to swim anymore. Yes, the school is supposed to keep us safe, but the school is also supposed to provide us with a well-rounded education, which includes athletic programs. '

## News Writing Contest Tips and Sample Story

Contest Director: Give one copy to each judge to use during critiquing/judging. Also, staple one copy of the contest and one copy of the contest tips to each student's returned entry. The purpose of the tips is to provide immediate feedback to students. However, it is not meant to replace written comments from the judge.

1. Open with a future lead under the present circumstances. In this instance, the story will be published before a student asks the school board to reinstate the swim season.

Freshman swimmer Nicole Oesterreicher will ask the school board to reinstate the fall 2023 varsity swim season on May 22 after administrators canceled it, citing a video that shows evidence of widespread hazing among swim team members.

2. Use direct quotes and get those quotes early in the story. After you introduce quotes, use the transition/quote formula. Be sure you attribute the quote. An attribution should be: subject of attribution followed by the verb of attribution ('Oesterreicher said,' not 'said Oesterreicher.') Avoid stacking quotes. Avoid putting two or more quotes from different people together without placing a transition between them. Also, make sure transitions provide information to help the action of the story and the quote does not repeat information revealed in the transition.

## transition

The head coach and assistant coach have been placed on unpaid leave for the duration of the investigation, and some varsity swimmers have been placed at the district's discipline campus.

## direct quote

'Students who don't feel safe can't perform at their highest potential,' superintendent Jorge Estevez said. 'As a result, we have decided that suspending the swim program for next fall is the best and safest approach for the students in our charge, particularly while the investigation by law enforcement into the alleged hazing continues.'

## transition

The video was shared around social media and depicts varsity members holding other students under water for extended periods of time. Varsity swimmers were allegedly initiating their younger team members.

## direct quote

'We were attacked after practice by a group of varsity swimmers,' sophomore swimmer Maggie Snowden said. 'Luckily, they didn't hold me underwater for long, but they did hold one of my friends down until she was choking and sick. I don't think they meant to physically hurt anyone, and I don't think they realized it was hazing. But it was scary, and it wasn't right.'

3. Use third person. Don't say 'our students' or 'our principal' or 'Some of our students.' Also, do not editorialize. For instance, you should not say 'This program probably won't work...' or 'The school should ...' without attributing that quote to someone. These statements are personal opinions and cannot be made in a news story.
4.  Note how in the earlier example of transition/quote, each quote is its own paragraph. Don't be afraid to use one- and two-sentence paragraphs. Journalistic writing consists mostly of one- and two-sentence paragraphs.
