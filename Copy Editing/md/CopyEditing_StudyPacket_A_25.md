McALLEN - Laredo residents are free to use their water after an eleven-day boil water notice expired earlier this week.

City officials announced the end of the warning and pledged to address the factors believed to have caused the cities water crisis. Those factors include illegal connections to the cities water system and decades-old pipes that must be replaced.

Laredo mayor Victor D. Treviño announced the city would start an amnesty program for illegal water connections to encourage residents to come forward about possible violations after the city identified over 200 bad or illegal connections throughout there investigation.

'Since there were over 200 violations found just in the central Laredo area alone, I believe that stands to reason that there are many more in the city.' Victor Treviño said during a news conference Jan. 8, 2025.

The city lifted the boil-water notice for the entire city on Monday after having it in place since January. 2. It was first put in place when Escherichia coli was discovered in the water. A city of more than 256 thousand people, Laredo is one of the most populous to issue a boil-water notice after Houston did so in 2022.

More than 30% of the city's water lines are over 50 years old, said Laredo city manager Joseph Neeb, adding that city officials intend to replace those pipes.
